# Phase 229 — SEC 13D/G regulatory cohort assignment by event-date proxy

**Date:** 2026-11-09  
**Disposition:** USE THE REPORTED EVENT DATE AS THE BEST AVAILABLE REGIME-TRIGGER PROXY; KEEP ACCEPTANCE DATETIME AS THE KNOWLEDGE CLOCK. PHASE 228'S WEIGHTED LAG CONCLUSIONS ARE NUMERICALLY STABLE.  
**Research class:** immutable-source reclassification audit; no returns or alpha test.

## Executive decision

Phase 228 split filings into pre/post regulatory cohorts using the **filing date**. That is acceptable as a descriptive filing-date split, but it is not the preferred assignment for studying which deadline regime applied to the event that generated the filing. The SEC's revised deadlines attach to acquisition, material-change, threshold, or reporting-period events; the filing date is the later disclosure outcome.

Phase 229 therefore adds a second, trigger-proxy classification using the Schedule cover-page/XML field **“Date of Event Which Requires Filing of this Statement.”** It is only a proxy: the field is not a verified trade timestamp, and for some 13G filers it represents a quarter-end reporting/threshold date rather than an individual transaction.

The reclassification changes exactly **one parsed filing** in the Phase 124 stratified sample:
- Accession: `0001554855-24-000055`
- Form: SC 13D/A
- Event date: 2024-01-31
- SEC filing date: 2024-02-08
- Filing-date split: post-2024-02-05
- Event-date-proxy split: pre-2024-02-05

The 13D/A event occurred before the revised 13D deadline regime began. The document itself describes an investment agreement dated January 31, 2024. The SEC acceptance header is 2024-02-08 16:33:22 ET. The audit does **not** determine whether the filing satisfied the prior “promptly” standard.

No 13G filing crosses the September 30, 2024 boundary in this sample. The 13G event-date and filing-date cohort summaries are therefore identical among the 348 parsed observations.

## 2. Correct regime-assignment principle

The SEC's adopting release states:
- Revised 13D initial and amendment deadlines apply from February 5, 2024.
- Revised 13G deadlines are not required before September 30, 2024; the old 13G deadlines continue through September 29, 2024.
- Structured-data compliance begins December 18, 2024, a separate format break.

For a filing generated by an event, the event/trigger date is the better cohort anchor than the later filing date. In this dataset, the Schedule event-date field is the best available proxy, but it is not a legal adjudication of the trigger for every filer or rule category.

The 13G family must remain especially cautious: Rules 13d-1(b), (c), and (d), plus Rules 13d-2(b), (c), and (d), have different triggers and deadlines. Only 68 structured 13G rows in the Phase 124 sample carry parsed rule designations. This audit does not infer a single deadline for all 13G filings and does not test compliance.

## 4. Interpretation

1. Phase 228's headline timing observations remain numerically stable after the event-date-proxy correction.
2. Its filing-date cohort should not be described as the definitive legal trigger regime for each observation.
3. One 13D/A filing crossed the cutoff between event and filing; it belongs in the pre-cutoff event cohort for this descriptive trigger-proxy comparison.
4. No 13G filing crossed the cutoff among parsed event dates, but 37 event dates remain unknown.
5. A cover-page event date is not proof of a trade date, actual acquisition date, or exact rule trigger in every case.
6. SEC acceptance datetime remains the knowledge clock. Never backdate a feature to the event date. For daily research, retain the existing conservative next-full-session rule.
7. The December 18, 2024 structured-data requirement is a separate format break, not a change in public-knowledge time.
8. This audit does not compute legal deadline compliance. In particular, pre-reform 13D amendments were subject to “promptly,” not a fixed numeric business-day threshold, and 13G deadlines depend on filer category and trigger.

## 5. Research decision and next gate

- Keep 13D initial and 13D/A separate.
- Keep 13G initial and 13G/A separate.
- Use event-date proxy, not filing date, for descriptive trigger-regime assignment where the event date is parseable.
- Keep unknown event dates unclassified; do not impute them from filing date.
- Preserve the original filing-date split as a separate descriptive view for comparison.
- Do not interpret this reclassification as evidence of alpha or regulatory compliance.
- Before any 13D-specific event study, require a verified SEC acceptance timestamp, effective-dated issuer/security identity, full active/delisted universe, amendment-state reconstruction, and next-session execution.
