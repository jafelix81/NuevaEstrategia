# Phase 28D — SEC 13D/13G PIT event audit

## Objective
Audit whether SEC Schedule 13D/13G filings can support a reproducible point-in-time event signal on the frozen 335-name universe. This is a data/PIT gate, not an alpha claim.

## Key hypothesis
A newly disclosed beneficial-ownership event may contain information about informed/control-oriented behavior. This is a hypothesis only. 13D/13G is not assumed to represent "smart money".

## PIT rule
- Filing date is the earliest public-availability timestamp used for a signal.
- Event/acquisition date is used only for lag diagnostics, never as the signal date.
- Current CIK→ticker mapping is used only to identify membership in the frozen universe; CIK is the primary security/issuer key.
- No report date, amendment date, later metadata, or future crosswalk is used to move an event backward in time.

## Coverage
Quarterly SEC full-index census from 2020Q4 through 2026Q3, with SEC submission-header resolution.

## Required diagnostics
- filing counts by quarter and form (13D, 13D/A, 13G, 13G/A)
- initial vs amendment counts
- issuer mapping rate into the frozen 335-name universe
- event-date availability
- filing-date minus event-date lag, including pre/post 2024 regime segmentation
- duplicate accession/issuer/date checks
- concentration of events by issuer

## Important regime break
SEC Rule 13d-1 was amended so initial Schedule 13D filings became due within five business days rather than ten, creating a meaningful pre/post-2024 reporting-lag regime. The audit must therefore not pool lag behavior blindly across the entire sample.

## Gate to alpha
Proceed only if historical coverage is adequate, issuer resolution is reproducible, event dates are sufficiently observable for lag diagnostics, and the PIT event rate is large enough for chronological OOS testing. If not, archive 13D/13G as a data-limited hypothesis rather than forcing an alpha test.
